Smacro

Privacy policy

Effective November 23, 2026

In short: Smacro doesn't collect your data. What you log stays on your iPhone and Apple Watch, in your own iCloud account if you use iCloud, and in Apple Health if you turn that on. To find foods, Smacro looks up what you scan or type, without knowing who you are. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. Purchases go through Apple, and Smacro never sees your payment details.

What you put into Smacro

Your food log, the foods and recipes you save, your daily goal, your weigh-ins, the body measurements you enter in the calorie calculator and your settings are stored on your iPhone. Weigh-ins and measurements are kept on the iPhone only, not in iCloud. The developer of Smacro never receives them and has no way to see them.

iCloud

If you're signed in to iCloud, Smacro keeps a copy of your log, foods and recipes in your private iCloud account using Apple's CloudKit. This lets your log come back if you reinstall the app or move to a new iPhone. That data is stored by Apple under Apple's privacy policy and can only be read by you. The developer can't access it. You can turn this off in the Settings app under your name, then iCloud.

Apple Health

Syncing with Apple Health is off until you turn it on in Smacro's settings, and you choose what to allow. When it's on, Smacro saves what you log (calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber and water, with the food's name) and your weigh-ins to Health, and reads weights from Health to show them in Goals. It reads nothing else.

What Smacro reads from Health stays on your iPhone. It's never put in iCloud, sent to Smacro's server, shared with anyone or used for advertising. Turning the sync off stops it. What's already in Health stays there until you remove it in the Health app.

Apple Watch

The Apple Watch app keeps a copy of your log on the watch. It's kept up to date through your iCloud account or, without iCloud, directly between your iPhone and your watch. Your goal and day start are sent from your iPhone to your watch. The watch app doesn't use Apple Health.

Looking up foods

When you scan or type a barcode, Smacro sends that barcode number to Smacro's server. When you search for a food, or write a line on the page, Smacro sends the words you've typed so far, so it can suggest foods as you go. The server looks them up in fatsecret's food database and sends back matching foods and their nutrition facts. Your name, your goal, your weigh-ins and the rest of your log are never sent.

The server has no accounts and doesn't know who you are. Its logs say only what kind of lookup was made and whether it worked, never what was scanned or typed. Like any website, it can see the request's internet address. It uses that in memory, only to limit how many lookups one address can make in a minute, and never saves it. It keeps each barcode or search and fatsecret's answer, not who asked, in memory for up to 24 hours, so the same one isn't looked up again.

fatsecret only sees requests from Smacro's server, never from your iPhone. The server runs on Fly.io, which handles its connections under Fly.io's privacy policy.

Camera and photos

Smacro uses the camera to read barcodes and nutrition labels. When you choose a photo of a label, only that photo is shared with the app. Both are read on your iPhone and are never uploaded or saved by Smacro.

Backup files

If you save a backup, Smacro creates a file with your log, foods, recipes and goal and hands it to you through the share sheet. Weigh-ins aren't in it. Where it goes from there, such as Files, AirDrop or email, is your choice.

Purchases

Smacro Pro and the looks, Smacritters and outfits in the Smacro Shop are sold through Apple's App Store using In-App Purchase. Apple handles the payment, your Apple Account and any refunds, under Apple's privacy policy. Smacro never receives your name, email address, payment details or Apple Account.

To know what you own, Smacro asks the App Store on your iPhone. It keeps a list of the items you own on your iPhone, so they don't show as locked while it checks again. Nothing about your purchases is sent to the developer or to Smacro's server.

Apple gives the developer sales reports with totals, such as how many of each item sold and in which countries, but not who bought them.

What Smacro doesn't do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No advertising, analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no selling or sharing of data.

Deleting your data

In Smacro, open settings, then Back up your log, then Delete all data. That erases your log, foods, recipes, goal, weigh-ins and measurements from your iPhone and from iCloud. If Apple Health sync is on, what Smacro saved there is removed too.

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your iPhone. To remove the copy in iCloud as well, open the Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage, and delete Smacro's data.

Children

Smacro doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes

If Smacro ever starts handling data differently, for example by adding a new service, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
